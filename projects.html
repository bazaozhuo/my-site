  • 01 Live · shipped

    Deutsch-Lernzimmer

    Web · German · study tool

    I built this for my own German course and then opened it up. It holds the 703 words I keep forgetting, the grammar points I got wrong, and a challenge that stops me from pretending I know them. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript — no backend, no framework.

    How it worksEvery word lives in eight data files, one per topic group. The vocabulary table, the word challenge and the daily five all read from that same list, so when I add a word it appears in three places at once.

  • 02 Planned · 4–5 days

    Silk & Bars

    Music · Chinese samples · English rap

    I want to put traditional Chinese instruments under an English rap verse. I'd record a short guzheng or erhu phrase myself, cut it across a 16-step grid, put a beat underneath, rap over it, and export the whole thing as one file.

    How it worksA real audio clock, not setInterval alone: a lookahead scheduler reads AudioContext.currentTime and queues each slice slightly early, so the grid still lines up after two minutes. The mix renders through OfflineAudioContext and encodes to WAV inside the page.

    Every sample gets a credit line — only my own recordings or public-domain material.

  • 03 Planned · 2–3 days

    Time Capsule

    Future · cryptography · no backend

    I want to write a letter to my future self and actually lock it. Password, unlock date, done. Before that date nobody can read it — not even the site, because the site never holds the key.

    How it worksPBKDF2 turns my passphrase into a key, AES-GCM encrypts the letter, and only the ciphertext is stored. If I forget the passphrase it is gone for good, and the page will say that out loud rather than pretend otherwise.

  • 04 Planned · 3–4 days

    Void Terminal

    Science fiction · parser · narrative

    I want to sit at the terminal of a ship that stopped answering. No mouse, no buttons — I type. I scan the decks, pull the black box, decrypt what is left of it, and open the right bay before the air runs out.

    How it worksA small command parser behind a state machine: split the line, resolve it against the current world state, print the answer with a typing delay. Commands unlock as the story moves, so it cannot be brute-forced.
